01 · Why we exist

We will hand more and more of our work to AI. That is mostly good. But delegating well depends on still being able to frame a problem, weigh what you are told, and make the call yourself.

ThinkGym trains that ability in three modes.

  • On your own

    Frame the problem and commit to a position before any help arrives.

  • With help

    Work it through with a coach that asks rather than tells.

  • About help

    Judge the help itself: when to rely on it, what to question, and when to set it aside.

02 · How the coach works

Six principles we build by.

Every part of ThinkGym is held to these. They decide what the coach will and won’t do.

  1. Commit first

    You state a position before any help. Thinking starts with you, not with a suggestion.

  2. Elicit, never suggest

    The coach draws your reasoning out with questions. It doesn’t slip its own answer in.

  3. Withhold solutions, not information

    Facts and context are fair game. The answer to your question is yours to reach.

  4. You never file anything

    You say and you judge. The machine does the bookkeeping.

  5. Difficulty is the product

    Delight is the delivery. The work stays hard; coming back to it should feel good.

  6. Measure what’s real

    We look at calibration and transfer: whether your confidence matches your accuracy, and whether the skill carries to new problems. Never self-report.

03 · Products

A room for real problems, and a daily rep.

Two ways to practise the same thing: thinking for yourself, with a coach that keeps you honest.

BYOQ

Bring Your Own Question

A thinking room for your own real problems.

Bring the question you’re actually facing: a job switch, a hard call at work. Think it through solo or as a group. The coach guides, challenges and facilitates, but it is never one of the thinkers. It keeps the notes, so you can pick up where you left off.

  • Your question
  • You, or your group
  • The coach, around the room
  • Notes, kept for later
Daily practice

Daily Quest

Short, fun practice that builds the habit.

A few minutes of hard, enjoyable reasoning each day. It keeps the skill in shape, and it teaches the coach how you reason, so its help fits you when the stakes are real.

What the coach won’t do

It helps you think. It won’t take on the errands and chores that belong to general assistants, and it won’t hand you the answer to your own question.

The long view

Over time, we want ThinkGym to be where important decisions are reasoned through, and later revisited against how they actually turned out.
